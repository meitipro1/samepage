---
title: Sealing and opening
description: Why neither side can read, copy or adjust to the other's account, and what the ticket is.
---

Both sides write before either can read. That is the whole point: an account written after reading the other one is a
reply, not an account.

## The seal

```text
seal = sha256(text + "|" + salt + "|" + lower_case(address))
```

The site makes a fresh salt with `crypto.randomUUID()` and hashes your exact text, the salt and your address in the
browser. Only the seal goes on chain. Each side seals once per round, and nobody can open before both have sealed.

- **A copied seal can never be opened.** The address is inside the hash. If one side copies the other's seal, the copier
  cannot produce text and salt that hash to it with their own address, so the open is refused and the copier misses the
  opening window.
- **Nothing to read early.** A hash of 1,200 characters and a 36 character salt gives nothing away.

## The ticket

Your ticket is the exact text and the salt. Without them your seal cannot be opened, and a side that cannot open misses
the window and loses its deposit. The site saves the ticket in your browser before you sign and offers it as a download.
Opening from another browser takes the downloaded file.

## Opening

When both have sealed, each side opens: the contract hashes the text and salt with the sender's address and stores the
text only if it matches the seal. The text is stored exactly as sent, line breaks included. Once both are open, the page
is ready to check, and both texts are public.

See [Hashing, with test vectors](/docs/reference/hashing) for the bytes, and the vectors both the contract and the site
are tested against.
